# Moisture Budget
By Sandro W. Lubis (PNNL)

The column moisture budget describes the temporal evolution of water vapor within an atmospheric column. In pressure coordinates, the local tendency of specific humidity, $\partial q/\partial t$, is governed by the combined effects of horizontal moisture advection, horizontal moisture convergence, vertical moisture transport, and diabatic moisture sources and sinks. Specifically, the first term on the right-hand side of Eq.~(\ref{eq:moisture_budget}) represents horizontal advection of moisture, the second term denotes horizontal moisture convergence, the third term corresponds to the flux-form vertical moisture advection, and the fourth term represents moisture loss or gain associated with phase-change and surface exchange processes, including condensation, raindrop evaporation in unsaturated air, and surface evaporation.

Under steady-state conditions, the atmospheric moisture storage term is assumed to be negligible, such that $\partial q/\partial t \approx 0$. In this limit, the column-integrated moisture budget reduces to a balance between total moisture flux convergence and net precipitation. Thus, the vertically integrated moisture flux convergence is approximately balanced by $P - E$, where $P$ is precipitation and $E$ is evaporation. Positive moisture flux convergence indicates a net accumulation of water vapor within the column and is therefore associated with positive net precipitation, whereas moisture flux divergence corresponds to reduced net precipitation or net drying of the column.
